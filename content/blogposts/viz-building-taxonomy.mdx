## What is this about?

  A small-multiple grid of simplified building outlines for selected cities, built with PyData, QuackOSM, and Metaflow from OpenStreetMap geometries.

  > **[#30DayMapChallenge | Day 3: Polygons | 2024](https://30daymapchallenge.com/)** (see: ["30DayMapChallenge" official Github repo](https://github.com/tjukanovt/30DayMapChallenge))
  >
  > Recently I saw a post by Daniel Gorokhov who illustrated building taxonomy of "interesting buildings" in Milano, Italy, Stockholm, Sweden and Amsterdam, Netherlands. Here is my extension for that post, with some more cities, mostly in USA, but also in Europe.
  >
  > <p>Chart type is <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_multiple">"small multiple" (a.k.a. "grid chart")</a>. How was it made? I run a script that loaded all mapped buildings within city area and counted exterior vertices (nodes) for all of them. Still, I did not explicitly count inner polygons, like courtyards or patios. Then, I took one hundred of buildings with the most nodes to plot them.</p>

## How do I read it?

  The chart is a **small multiple** (grid chart). Each city is one panel. Inside each panel, the 100 buildings with the most exterior vertices are drawn as simplified outlines.

  Complexity here means vertex count on the exterior ring, not architectural fame. Courtyards and other inner rings are not counted explicitly.
  Under this rule, "interesting" shapes often repeat: housing projects, industrial ponds, fuel storages, dockside structures, and similar construction patterns.
  Some truly complex buildings are also poorly mapped in OSM and need case-by-case handling (multi-buildings, connections, and similar edge cases).

## Why did I make it?

  I wanted to extend Daniel Gorokhov's Milano / Stockholm / Amsterdam taxonomy idea to a wider city set for [#30DayMapChallenge](https://30daymapchallenge.com/) Day 3 (Polygons), and to keep the pipeline reproducible in Python rather than as a one-off illustration.

## What's under the hood?

  Pipeline sketch: load OSM buildings for each city with QuackOSM, count exterior vertices, keep the top 100 per city, plot the small multiples with Matplotlib, orchestrated in Metaflow.
