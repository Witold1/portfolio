## What is this about?

> **[#30DayChartChallenge | OECD Data Day](https://github.com/30DayChartChallenge)**
>
> Recently I saw a wonderful #30DayChartChallenge OECD (Data Day) visualization of land use patterns by country, made by [Mireia Camacho](https://es.linkedin.com/in/mireia-camacho-695475143). I liked it a lot, so I decided to reproduce it with Python and slightly make it out.
>
> As usual, pure #python and #matplotlib were used. Chart type is unusual: semantically it resembles a mix of pie chart and bar chart, but most people call it a petal, or polygonic petal, chart. Still, here is a small multiple with such petal sub-charts.
>
> What changes have I made?
>
> <ol class="mdx-list-circled">
>   <li>Firstly, I "zoomed" observations in, following their distribution. Why? Because the sum of shares in each chart can't exceed 100%, most observations lie below the 50-60% level. Thus, I felt an axes range from 0% to 100% was redundant, because it defocused the viewpoint from actual data points to empty spaces.</li>
>   <li>Secondly, I sorted sub-charts by total area of countries. It allows an additional hidden dimension, because percentages of area are relative numbers that can be tricky if viewers would like to compare countries. Some 10% of China's land may exceed some 50% of Thailand's land.</li>
>   <li>Thirdly, I added annotation of "land use specialization", or the biggest petal, to sub-charts. It should enrich previous points and allow more freedom for cross-country comparison.</li>
>   <li>The last but quite important, I added enumeration to sub-charts to retrieve information faster.</li>
> </ol>
>
> What else could we work on?
>
> - More attention to dynamics. Some observations, with no regard to quality, are available decades in advance. We better get some questions stated, but even from such a general prism, between 1992 (random point) and 2021 (most recent available), we could see huge deforestation in Brazil, Indonesia, Congo, Peru, and many other countries.
> - Practices of accessibility. Under a full gray filter, colored elements of the sub-chart are not contrast enough, so we could add more accessibility-friendly methods not to rely on colors (for example, either patterns and dash styles to petals or an additional legend with location of petals).
>

## How to read a cell

| Element | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Cell | One country (rank by total land area) |
| Four petals | ARABLE / FOREST / MEAD / OTHER |
| Petal length | Category share among the four OECD % values |
| Rings | 10% / 25% / 50% guides |
| Subtitle under name | Largest category + absolute km² |

Hover a country for the breakdown; click a colored category in the subtitle to highlight that petal across all flowers.

## Data

OECD land-use measures, prepared as:

* `data/oecd_land_use_2021.csv` - long-format dump (notebook source)
* `data/land_use_top50.json` - wide table for the web app
* `data/presets.meta.json` - Top 30 / Top 50 captions

See the repo’s `data/README.md` for measure definitions and retrieval notes. OECD/FAO source terms still apply beside the prepared-table license.
