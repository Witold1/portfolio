## What is this about?

From Russian Empire subjects to Soviet citizens, this project illustrates population change with the [1897](https://yulianovozhilova.ru/perepis1897-doc) and [1926](https://yulianovozhilova.ru/per1926) censuses via maps and tables.

The Imperial census asked about social estate, native language, and religious affiliation. The Soviet census asked about self-declared nationality (_narodnost_) - and native language (not included in this extract). Backbone GIS comes from [Sablin et al., doi:10.11588/DATA/10064](https://doi.org/10.11588/data/10064).

## Try it yourself

Open the live explorer: [LINK - "Transcultural Empire Peoplespace"](https://witold1.github.io/transcultural-empire-dataset/). Source on [GitHub](https://github.com/Witold1/transcultural-empire-dataset).

## Views

| View | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Map | Province choropleth, fixed VisQuill lens, composition panels, side panel |
| Data table | Full DBF attributes (language / religion / estate / nationality); click a row to open that unit on the map |

Shared **1897 · 1926** year toggle and **Modern · Historical** basemap toggle across both views. Historical defaults to [OpenHistoricalMap](https://www.openhistoricalmap.org/) dated to the selected census; Modern is OpenFreeMap Liberty.

## Years

| Year | Units | Population (manifest) | Choropleth examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1897 | 99 (89 with census) | ~126M | urban %, sex ratio, density, top language / religion share |
| 1926 | 67 | ~149M | total / urban / rural, sex ratio, density, top nationality share |

## Stack

Vite + TypeScript explorer (MapLibre, VisQuill); Python prep (`pyshp`, `shapely`) rebuilds `data/processed/` GeoJSON, dictionaries, and tables from the Sablin shapefiles.

Further census context: [Explanatory Notes (1926)](https://web.archive.org/web/20191013235801/http://www.demoscope.ru/weekly/2006/0267/arxiv04.php) and the [List and Dictionary of Nationalities (1927)](https://web.archive.org/web/20240619200541/https://www.demoscope.ru/weekly/knigi/slovar/slovar_1926.pdf) via Demoscope Weekly.
